General Intake and Safety
Basic Patient Information
Initial Assessment Questions
Caller: "Are you calling for yourself or for someone else?" If calling for someone else, ask: "What is your relationship to the patient?"
Name: "What is the patient's full name?"
Age: "How old is the patient?" For a young child, collect age in years and months when useful.
Date Of Birth: "What is the patient's date of birth?"
Sex / Gender: "What sex was the patient assigned at birth?" Ask gender identity only when relevant to the patient record or care context.
Callback Number: "What is the best phone number to reach you if the call disconnects?"
Current Location: "Where are you right now?"
Height And Weight: "Do you know the patient's approximate height and weight?" Ask when relevant.
Medical Conditions: "Does the patient have any important medical conditions?"
Medicines: "What medicines is the patient currently taking?"
Allergies: "Does the patient have any medication or other serious allergies?" If Yes, ask: "What reaction do they have?"
Recent Care: "Has the patient recently been in the hospital, had surgery, or seen a doctor for this problem?"
Available Measurements: "Do you have any recent measurements such as temperature, blood pressure, pulse, oxygen level, or blood sugar?"
Pregnancy: Ask only when applicable: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?" If pregnant, ask gestational age and estimated due date when relevant.
Emergency / 911 Symptoms
Initial Assessment Questions
Symptoms: "What is the most serious symptom?"
Airway: "Are they breathing?" (e.g., Yes, No) R/O: airway blockage, respiratory arrest.
Breathing: "Is there difficulty breathing?" (e.g., Yes, No, Unknown; severity) R/O: respiratory distress.
Circulation: "Are you (they) feeling weak?" (e.g., Yes, No, Unknown; severity) R/O: shock.
Bleeding: "Is there any bleeding?" If Yes, ask: "How bad is the bleeding?" (e.g., actively dripping or spurting) R/O: hemorrhage.
Conscious: "Are they awake and alert?" (e.g., alert-oriented, confused, lethargic, stuporous, comatose) R/O: altered mental status, coma.
Behavioral Danger / Threat to Others
Initial Assessment Questions
Danger Now: "Are you in danger right now?" If Yes, ask: "What is happening right now?"
Concern: "What happened that made you call today?"
Injuries: "Is anyone injured?" If Yes, ask: "Please describe them."
Attempt: "Has your teen (or child) tried to harm anyone?"
Threat: "Has your teen (or child) threatened to hurt anyone?"
Onset: "When did the behavior begin?"
Recurrent Symptoms: "Has your teen (or child) ever done this before?" If Yes, ask: "When was the last time?" and "What happened that time?"
Therapist: "Does your teen have a counselor or therapist?" If Yes, ask: "When was the last time your child was seen? Have you spoken with the counselor regarding your concerns?"
Current Behavior: "What is your teen (or child) doing right now?"
Cardiovascular
High Blood Pressure
Initial Assessment Questions
Blood Pressure: "What is your blood pressure?" "Did you take at least two measurements 5 minutes apart?"
Onset: "When did you take your blood pressure?"
How: "How did you take your blood pressure?" (e.g., automatic home BP monitor, visiting nurse)
History: "Do you have a history of high blood pressure?"
Medicines: "Are you taking any medicines for blood pressure?" "Have you missed any doses recently?"
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any symptoms?" (e.g., blurred vision, chest pain, difficulty breathing, headache, weakness)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Low Blood Pressure
Initial Assessment Questions
Blood Pressure: "What is your blood pressure?" "Did you take at least two measurements 5 minutes apart?"
Onset: "When did you take your blood pressure?"
How: "How did you take your blood pressure?" (e.g., visiting nurse, automatic home BP monitor)
History: "Do you have a history of low blood pressure?" "What is your blood pressure normally?"
Medicines: "Are you taking any medicines for blood pressure?" If Yes, ask: "Have they been changed recently?"
Pulse Rate: "Do you know what your pulse rate is?"
Other Symptoms: "Have you been sick recently?" "Have you had a recent injury?"
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Palpitations / Abnormal Heartbeat
Initial Assessment Questions
Description: "Please describe your heart rate or heartbeat that you are having." (e.g., fast/slow, regular/irregular, skipped or extra beats, palpitations)
Onset: "When did it start?" (e.g., minutes, hours, days)
Duration: "How long does it last?" (e.g., seconds, minutes, hours)
Pattern: "Does it come and go, or has it been constant since it started?" "Does it get worse with exertion?" "Are you feeling it now?"
Tap: "Using your hand, can you tap out what you are feeling on a chair or table in front of you, so that I can hear?" Not all patients can do this.
Heart Rate: "Can you tell me your heart rate?" "How many beats in 15 seconds?" Not all patients can do this.
Recurrent Symptom: "Have you ever had this before?" If Yes, ask: "When was the last time?" and "What happened that time?"
Cause: "What do you think is causing the palpitations?"
Cardiac History: "Do you have any history of heart disease?" (e.g., heart attack, angina, bypass surgery, angioplasty, arrhythmia)
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., dizziness, chest pain, sweating, difficulty breathing)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Respiratory and Infectious
Difficulty Breathing / Shortness of Breath
Initial Assessment Questions
Respiratory Status: "Describe your breathing." (e.g., wheezing, shortness of breath, unable to speak, severe coughing)
Onset: "When did this breathing problem begin?"
Pattern: "Does the difficult breathing come and go, or has it been constant since it started?"
Severity: "How bad is your breathing?" (e.g., mild, moderate, severe)
Mild: No SOB at rest, mild SOB with walking, speaks normally in sentences, can lie down, no retractions, pulse < 100.
Moderate: SOB at rest, SOB with minimal exertion and prefers to sit, cannot lie down flat, speaks in phrases, mild retractions, audible wheezing, pulse 100 to 120.
Severe: Very SOB at rest, speaks in single words, struggling to breathe, sitting hunched forward, retractions, pulse > 120.
Recurrent Symptom: "Have you had difficulty breathing before?" If Yes, ask: "When was the last time?" and "What happened that time?"
Cardiac History: "Do you have any history of heart disease?" (e.g., heart attack, angina, bypass surgery, angioplasty)
Lung History: "Do you have any history of lung disease?" (e.g., pulmonary embolus, asthma, emphysema)
Cause: "What do you think is causing the breathing problem?"
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., chest pain, cough, dizziness, fever, runny nose)
O2 Saturation Monitor: "Do you use an oxygen saturation monitor (pulse oximeter) at home?" If Yes, ask: "What is your reading (oxygen level) today?" "What is your usual oxygen saturation reading?" (e.g., 95%)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Travel: "Have you traveled out of the country in the last month?" (e.g., travel history, exposures)
Cough
Initial Assessment Questions
Onset: "When did the cough begin?"
Severity: "How bad is the cough today?"
Sputum: "Describe the color of your sputum." (e.g., none, dry cough; clear, white, yellow, green)
Hemoptysis: "Are you coughing up any blood?" If Yes, ask: "How much?" (e.g., flecks, streaks, tablespoons)
Difficulty Breathing: "Are you having difficulty breathing?" If Yes, ask: "How bad is it?" (e.g., mild, moderate, severe)
Mild: No SOB at rest, mild SOB with walking, speaks normally in sentences, can lie down, no retractions, pulse < 100.
Moderate: SOB at rest, SOB with minimal exertion and prefers to sit, cannot lie down flat, speaks in phrases, mild retractions, audible wheezing, pulse 100 to 120.
Severe: Very SOB at rest, speaks in single words, struggling to breathe, sitting hunched forward, retractions, pulse > 120.
Fever: "Do you have a fever?" If Yes, ask: "What is your temperature, how was it measured, and when did it start?"
Cardiac History: "Do you have any history of heart disease?" (e.g., heart attack, congestive heart failure)
Lung History: "Do you have any history of lung disease?" (e.g., pulmonary embolus, asthma, emphysema)
PE Risk Factors: "Do you have a history of blood clots?" Other risk factors include recent major surgery, recent prolonged travel, and being bedridden.
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., runny nose, wheezing, chest pain)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Travel: "Have you traveled out of the country in the last month?" (e.g., travel history, exposures)

COVID-19 Exposure - Child
Initial Assessment Questions
Covid-19 Patient: "Who is the person with confirmed or suspected COVID-19 infection that your child was exposed to?"
Place Of Contact: "Where was your child when they were exposed to the patient?" (e.g., home, school, child care)
Type Of Contact: "What type of contact was there?" (e.g., talking to, sitting next to, same room, same building) Source note: within 6 feet (2 meters) for 15 minutes is considered close contact.
Duration Of Contact: "How long were you or your child in contact with the COVID-19 patient?" (e.g., minutes, hours, live with the patient) Source note: a total of 15 minutes or more over a 24-hour period is considered close contact.
Mask: "Was your child wearing a mask?" Source note: wearing a mask reduces the risk of an otherwise close contact.
Date Of Contact: "When did your child have contact with a COVID-19 patient?" (e.g., how many days ago)
Symptoms: "Does your child have any symptoms?" No symptoms required to use this question set.
Higher Risk For Complications With Covid-19: "Does your child have any chronic medical problems?" (e.g., heart or lung disease, diabetes, asthma, cancer, weak immune system)
Vaccines: "Is your child vaccinated against COVID-19?" If eligible, ask: "Have they received a booster shot?"
Influenza Follow-Up
Initial Assessment Questions
Diagnosis Confirmation: "When was the influenza diagnosed?" "By whom?" "Did you get a test for it?"
Influenza Medicines: "Were you prescribed any medicines for the influenza?" (e.g., zanamivir [Relenza], oseltamivir [Tamiflu])
Symptoms: "What is your main symptom or concern?" (e.g., cough, fever, shortness of breath, muscle aches)
Onset: "When did the symptoms start?"
Cough: "Do you have a cough?" If Yes, ask: "How bad is the cough?"
Fever: "Do you have a fever?" If Yes, ask: "What is your temperature, how was it measured, and when did it start?"
Breathing Difficulty: "Are you having any difficulty breathing?" (e.g., normal; shortness of breath, wheezing, unable to speak)
High Risk For Complications: "Do you have any chronic medical problems?" (e.g., asthma, heart or lung disease, obesity, weak immune system)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
O2 Saturation Monitor: "Do you use an oxygen saturation monitor (pulse oximeter) at home?" If Yes, ask: "What is your reading (oxygen level) today?" "What is your usual oxygen saturation reading?" (e.g., 95%)
Gastrointestinal
Abdominal / Stomach Pain
Initial Assessment Questions
Location: "Where does it hurt?"
Radiation: "Does the pain shoot anywhere else?" (e.g., chest, back)
Onset: "When did the pain begin?" (e.g., minutes, hours or days ago)
Sudden: "Gradual or sudden onset?"
Pattern: "Does the pain come and go, or is it constant?" Comes and goes means the pain is intermittent and goes away completely between bouts. Constant means the pain never goes away completely.
If It Comes And Goes: "How long does it last?" "Do you have pain now?"
If Constant: "Is it getting better, staying the same, or getting worse?"
Severity: "How bad is the pain?" (e.g., Scale 1-10; mild, moderate, or severe)
Mild (1-3): Does not interfere with normal activities, abdomen soft and not tender to touch.
Moderate (4-7): Interferes with normal activities or awakens from sleep, abdomen tender to touch.
Severe (8-10): Excruciating pain, doubled over, unable to do any normal activities.
Recurrent Symptom: "Have you ever had this type of stomach pain before?" If Yes, ask: "When was the last time?" and "What happened that time?"
Cause: "What do you think is causing the stomach pain?" (e.g., gallstones, recent abdominal surgery)
Relieving/Aggravating Factors: "What makes it better or worse?" (e.g., antacids, bending or twisting motion, bowel movement)
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., back pain, diarrhea, fever, urination pain, vomiting)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Stool Color Change
Initial Assessment Questions
Color: "What color is your stool?" "Is that color in part or all of the stool?" (e.g., black, clay-colored, green, red)
Onset: "When did you first notice this color change?"
Cause: "Have you eaten any food or taken any medicine of this color?"
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., abdomen pain, diarrhea, fever, yellow eyes or skin)
Vomiting
Initial Assessment Questions
Vomiting Severity: "How many times have you vomited in the past 24 hours?"
Mild: 1 to 2 times/day.
Moderate: 3 to 5 times/day, decreased oral intake without significant weight loss or symptoms of dehydration.
Severe: 6 or more times/day, vomits everything or nearly everything, with significant weight loss or symptoms of dehydration.
Onset: "When did the vomiting begin?"
Fluids: "What fluids or food have you vomited up today?" "Have you been able to keep any fluids down?"
Abdomen Pain: "Are you having any abdomen pain?" If Yes, ask: "How bad is it and what does it feel like?" (e.g., crampy, dull, intermittent, constant)
Diarrhea: "Is there any diarrhea?" If Yes, ask: "How many times today?"
Contacts: "Is there anyone else in the family with the same symptoms?"
Cause: "What do you think is causing your vomiting?"
Hydration Status: "Any signs of dehydration?" (e.g., dry mouth [not only dry lips], too weak to stand) "When did you last urinate?"
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., fever, headache, vertigo, vomiting blood or coffee grounds, recent head injury)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Vomiting Blood
Initial Assessment Questions
Appearance Of Blood: "What does the blood look like?" (e.g., pink, red blood, coffee-grounds)
Amount: "How much blood was lost?" (e.g., few streaks or strands, tablespoon, cup)
Vomiting Blood: "How many times did it happen?" or "How many times in the past 24 hours?"
Vomiting Without Blood: "How many times in the past 24 hours?"
Onset: "When did vomiting of blood begin?"
Cause: "What do you think is causing the vomiting of blood?"
Blood Thinners: "Do you take any blood thinners?" (e.g., aspirin, clopidogrel / Plavix, warfarin / Coumadin, heparin) Other strong blood thinners listed in the source include Arixtra (fondaparinux), Eliquis (apixaban), Pradaxa (dabigatran), and Xarelto (rivaroxaban).
Dehydration: "Are there any signs of dehydration?" "When was the last time you urinated?" "Do you feel dizzy?"
Abdomen Pain: "Are you having any abdomen pain?" If Yes, ask: "What does it feel like?" (e.g., crampy, dull, intermittent, constant)
Diarrhea: "Is there any diarrhea?" If Yes, ask: "How many times today?"
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., fever, blood in stool)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Behavioral Health and Substance Use
Alcohol Use / Alcohol-Related Concern
Initial Assessment Questions
Alcohol Use: "Do you drink alcohol, including beer, wine or hard liquor?"
How Often: "How many days per week do you typically drink alcohol?"
How Much: "How many drinks do you typically have on days when you drink?" (1.5 oz hard liquor [one shot or jigger; 45 ml], 5 oz wine [small glass; 150 ml], 12 oz beer [one can; 360 ml])
Most: "What is the most that you have had to drink on any one occasion in the last month?"
Last 24 Hours: "Have you had a drink within the last 24 hours?"
Drinking Problem: "Do you have or have you ever had an alcohol drinking problem?"
Drug Problem: "Are you using any other drugs?" (e.g., cocaine, prescription medicines)
Symptoms: "What symptoms are you currently experiencing?" (e.g., none, tremors or shakiness, abdomen pain, vomiting, blackout spells)
Treatment Program: "Have you ever gone through an alcohol use treatment program?"
Therapist: "Do you have a counselor or therapist?" If Yes, ask: "What is their name?"
Support: "Who is with you now?" "Who do you live with?" "Do you have family or friends who you can talk to?" "Are you a member of Alcoholics Anonymous?"
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Suicidal Thoughts / Self-Harm Risk
Initial Assessment Questions
Main Concern: "What happened that made you call today?"
Risk Of Harm - Suicidal Ideation: "Do you ever have thoughts of hurting or killing yourself?" (e.g., yes, no, no but preoccupation with thoughts about death)
Wish To Be Dead: "Have you wished you were dead or wished you could go to sleep and not wake up?"
Intent: "Have you had any thoughts of hurting or killing yourself?" If Yes, continue.
Plan: "Have you thought about how you might do this?" "Do you have a specific plan for how you would do this?" (e.g., gun, knife, overdose, no plan)
Access: If there is a plan, ask: "Do you have access to it?" (e.g., pills, gun in house, knife in kitchen)
Risk Of Harm - Suicide Attempt: "Have you tried to harm yourself recently?" If Yes, ask: "When was this?" "What type of harm was tried?"
Risk Of Harm - Suicidal Behavior: "Have you ever done anything, started to do anything, or prepared to do anything to end your life?" (e.g., collected pills, bought a gun, wrote a suicide note, cut yourself, started but changed your mind)
Events And Stressors: "Has there been any new stress or recent changes in your life?" (e.g., death of loved one, homelessness, negative event, relationship breakup, work)
Functional Impairment: "How have things been going for you overall? Have you had more difficulty than usual doing your normal daily activities?" (e.g., better, same, worse; self-care, school, work, interactions)
Support: "Who is with you now?" "Who do you live with?" "Do you have family or friends who you can talk to?"
Therapist: "Do you have a counselor or therapist?" If Yes, ask: "What is their name?"
Alcohol Use Or Substance Use: "Do you drink alcohol or use any illegal drugs, or prescription drugs in ways other than prescribed?"
Other: "Do you have any other physical symptoms right now?" (e.g., fever)
Pregnancy Or Postpartum: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?" "Were you recently pregnant?" "When did you give birth?"
Injuries, Wounds, and Exposures
Allergic Reaction
Initial Assessment Questions
Symptoms: "What is the most serious symptom?"
Airway: "Are they breathing?" (e.g., Yes, No) R/O: airway blockage, respiratory arrest.
Breathing: "Is there difficulty breathing?" (e.g., Yes, No; wheezing, unable to complete a sentence) R/O: respiratory distress.
Circulation: "Are you feeling weak?" (e.g., Yes, No, Unknown; severity) If Yes, ask: "Can you stand and walk normally?" R/O: shock.
Swallowing: "Can you swallow?" (e.g., Yes, No; food, fluid, saliva)
Onset: "When did the reaction start?" (e.g., minutes, hours ago)
Substance: "What are you reacting to?" "When did the contact occur?"
Previous Reaction: "Have you ever reacted to it before?" If Yes, ask: "What happened that time?"
Epinephrine: "Do you have an epinephrine autoinjector (e.g., EpiPen) or epinephrine nasal spray (Neffy)?"
Animal Bite / Scratch
Initial Assessment Questions
Appearance: "What does it look like?" (e.g., abrasion, bruise, puncture)
Size: "How big is the bite?" (e.g., inches, cm; or compare to size of coin, pea, grape, ping pong ball)
Location: "Where is the bite located?"
Onset: "When did the bite happen?" (e.g., minutes, hours ago)
Animal: "What type of animal caused the bite?" "Is the injury from a bite or a claw?" If dog or cat, ask: "Was it a pet or a stray?" "Was it acting ill or behaving strangely?"
Rabies Vaccine: For dog or cat bites: "Do you know if the pet is vaccinated against rabies?" (e.g., yes, no, overdue for rabies shot, unknown)
Circumstances: "Tell me how this happened."
Tetanus: "When was your last tetanus booster?"
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Body Fluid Exposure
Initial Assessment Questions
Description: "Please describe what happened." (e.g., spitting, splash, touching)
Type And Amount Of Fluid: "What type of body fluid was it and how much?" (e.g., saliva, blood, vomit; a few drops, a splash)
Route Of Exposure: "What part of your body was exposed?" (e.g., eye, mouth, intact skin)
Date-Time Of Exposure: "When did this exposure occur?"
Source Person HIV Positive: "Is the other person HIV positive?" (e.g., yes, no, unknown)
Source Person Hepatitis Positive: "Is the other person hepatitis positive?" (e.g., yes, no, unknown)
Bruising / Easy Bruising
Initial Assessment Questions
Appearance Of Bruise: "Describe the bruise."
Size: "How large is the bruise?"
Number: "How many bruises are there?"
Location: "Where is the bruise located?"
Onset: "How long ago did the bruise occur?"
Cause: "What do you think caused the bruise?"
Medical History: "Do you have any medical problems that can cause easy bruising or bleeding?" (e.g., leukemia, liver disease, recent chemotherapy)
Blood Thinners: "Do you take any blood thinners?" (e.g., aspirin, clopidogrel / Plavix, warfarin / Coumadin, heparin) Other strong blood thinners listed in the source include Arixtra (fondaparinux), Eliquis (apixaban), Pradaxa (dabigatran), and Xarelto (rivaroxaban).
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., weakness, dizziness, pain, fever, nosebleed, blood in urine/stool)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Burn
Initial Assessment Questions
Onset: "When did it happen?" If it happened less than 3 hours ago, ask: "Did you apply cool water?"
Location: "Where is the burn located?"
Burn Size: "How large is the burn?" Source note: the palm, not including fingers, is roughly 0.5% of total body surface area.
Severity Of The Burn: "Are there any blisters?" "What size are they?" (e.g., quarter equals 1 inch or 2.5 cm) "Are any of the blisters broken, open, or wrinkled?"
Mechanism: "Tell me how it happened."
Pain: "Is there any pain?" If Yes, ask: "How bad is the pain?" (Scale 0-10; or none, mild, moderate, severe)
None (0): No pain.
Mild (1-3): Does not interfere with normal activities.
Moderate (4-7): Interferes with normal activities or awakens from sleep.
Severe (8-10): Excruciating pain, unable to do any normal activities.
Inhalation Injury: "Were you inside an enclosed space with heat and smoke?" If Yes, ask: "Do you have any cough or difficulty breathing?"
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., headache, nausea)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Cast Concern
Initial Assessment Questions
Cast Location: "Where is the cast?" (e.g., left lower leg, right arm)
Cast Symptom: "What is the main symptom you are concerned about?" (e.g., color change, numbness, pain)
Onset: "When did the symptom start?" "Is it getting worse?"
When Casted: "When was the cast put on?"
Injury: "What is the underlying injury?" (e.g., fracture, torn ligament, surgery)
Pain: "Is there any pain?" If Yes, ask: "How bad is it?" (Scale 1-10; or mild, moderate, severe)
Mild (1-3): Does not interfere with normal activities.
Moderate (4-7): Interferes with normal activities (e.g., work or school) or awakens from sleep.
Severe (8-10): Excruciating pain, unable to do any normal activities.
Other Symptoms: "Do you have any other symptoms?" (e.g., difficulty breathing, fever)
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Chest Injury
Initial Assessment Questions
Mechanism: "How did the injury happen?"
Onset: "When did the injury happen?" (e.g., minutes, hours, days ago)
Location: "Where on the chest is the injury located?"
Appearance: "What does the injury look like?"
Bleeding: "Is there any bleeding now?" If Yes, ask: "How long has it been bleeding?"
Severity: "Any difficulty with breathing?"
Size: For cuts, bruises, or swelling: "How large is it?" (e.g., inches or centimeters)
Pain: "Is there any pain?" If Yes, ask: "How bad is the pain?" (Scale 0-10; none, mild, moderate, severe)
None (0): No pain.
Mild (1-3): Does not interfere with normal activities.
Moderate (4-7): Interferes with normal activities or awakens from sleep.
Severe (8-10): Excruciating pain, unable to do any normal activities.
Tetanus: For any breaks in the skin: "When was your last tetanus booster?"
Pregnancy: "Is there any chance you are pregnant?" "When was your last menstrual period?"
Nose Injury
Initial Assessment Questions
Mechanism: "How did the injury happen?"
Onset: "When did the injury happen?" (e.g., minutes, hours ago)
Location: "What part of the nose is injured?"
Appearance Of Injury: "What does the nose look like?"

Emergency First Aid
Immediate Actions While Waiting for Emergency Services
Source note: this section gives short, one-step-at-a-time actions to talk a caller through during a life-threatening emergency, while 911 is being called or is en route. Always tell the caller to call (or have someone call) 911 first. These are general, widely-taught basic first aid steps, not a substitute for EMS or a doctor. Never recommend a specific medication or dose unless the caller already has their own prescribed device (e.g. their own EpiPen, their own nitroglycerin, their own inhaler) for that use.

Severe Bleeding: "Press down firmly on the wound with a clean cloth, towel, or your hand." "Keep steady, firm pressure — don't lift it to check." If on a limb and bleeding doesn't stop: "If you can, raise the injured area above the level of the heart while keeping pressure on it." "Do not remove any object that's stuck in the wound — hold it in place and apply pressure around it instead." "Keep them still, lying down if possible, and keep them warm."

Choking: "Is the person able to cough, speak, or breathe at all?" If they can cough or speak, encourage them to keep coughing. If they cannot breathe, cough, or make sound: "Call 911 immediately if not already on the line." Talk the caller through back blows and abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver) only if they indicate they know how or are willing to be walked through it, one step at a time; otherwise keep them on the line with 911 guidance.

Severe Allergic Reaction / Anaphylaxis: "Do they have their own epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) or epinephrine nasal spray (Neffy) with them?" If yes: "Use it now, exactly as prescribed." "Have them lie flat and raise their legs, unless they're having trouble breathing — in that case, let them sit up in whatever position is most comfortable for breathing." "If symptoms don't improve or they have another auto-injector, a second dose can be given after about 5-15 minutes if instructed by EMS."

Chest Pain (possible heart attack): "Have them sit or lie down in a comfortable position and stay as still and calm as possible." "If they have their own prescribed nitroglycerin, they can take it as prescribed." "If they are not allergic to aspirin and are able to safely chew and swallow, chewing one adult aspirin (325 mg) can be helpful — but do not give aspirin if there's any known allergy, bleeding disorder, or if EMS advises against it." "Loosen any tight clothing." "Keep them talking to you and stay on the line."

Stroke Symptoms: "Note the time symptoms started if you can — this matters a lot for treatment." "Keep them safe and comfortable, lying down with their head slightly elevated if possible." "Do not give them anything to eat or drink — swallowing may be affected." "Stay with them and keep talking to them, and keep them calm."

Seizure: "Do not hold them down or restrain their movements." "Clear the area around them of anything hard or sharp they could hit." "Do not put anything in their mouth." "If possible, gently turn them onto their side once the shaking stops or slows, to help keep their airway clear." "Time how long the seizure lasts if you can." "Stay with them until they're fully alert or help arrives."

Not Breathing / No Pulse: "Is the person breathing normally?" If not breathing normally or unresponsive: "If you know CPR, start chest compressions now — push hard and fast in the center of the chest." "If you don't know CPR, 911 dispatch can talk you through hands-only CPR — stay on that line." "If an AED (defibrillator) is nearby, have someone bring it."

Severe Burn: "Cool the burn with cool (not ice-cold) running water for several minutes." "Do not apply ice, butter, or ointments." "Do not break any blisters." "Remove any tight clothing or jewelry near the burn before it swells, if it can be done without hurting them further." "Cover loosely with a clean, dry cloth."

Suspected Overdose / Poisoning: "Do you know what was taken and roughly how much?" "Keep them awake and sitting up if possible, unless they're vomiting — then turn them on their side." "Do not try to make them vomit unless specifically told to by 911 or poison control." "If available, note the substance/container for when EMS arrives."
